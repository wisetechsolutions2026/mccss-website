Our journey

From flood relief to lasting empowerment

What began as relief for slum dwellers in North Chennai grew into a movement for rights, health, and dignity.

1965

Relief & foundation

Founded to give relief to slum dwellers in North Chennai during floods, fires and cyclones, later expanding into socio-political education for the poor.

1974 – 2001

Primary health care

Primary health care and health education, including the Urban Comprehensive Community Health Service Centre (1976–1991) with Madras Medical College, providing field training for medical students.

1980s

Community organisation

'Hutment Coordination' addressed evictions and awareness of rights. By 1983 the focus was on people's movements and policy change; in 1987, on urbanisation and globalisation. Family Counselling Centre and Short Stay Homes began for destitute women and children.

1991 – 2008

HIV/AIDS & health programmes

AIDS Awareness and Prevention (1991), Women in Prostitution Intervention with TANSACS (1994), STD clinic for 2,500+ beneficiaries (1995), Continuum of Care for People Living with AIDS (1997), and a programme for the Transgender Community (2008).

1996 – 2003

Sangams & Women's Department

Sangams took up the cause of violence against women, leading to a Women's Department recognised by the Tamil Nadu Corporation for Development of Women in 2003.

2004 – 2009

Anti-human trafficking

Projects supported by US AID and Geneva Global led to the 'Ujjawala Scheme' (2009) from the Ministry of Women and Child Development. MCCSS also briefly ran Night Shelters for rag pickers with the Corporation of Chennai.

2012

Homes for seniors & children

An Integrated Complex of Special Homes for Senior Citizens and Children, sponsored by the Government of Tamil Nadu — 25 residents per home with free accommodation, food and full-time care.
